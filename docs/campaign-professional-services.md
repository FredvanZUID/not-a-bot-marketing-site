## 1. Campaign Objective and KPIs

### Primary Objective
Generate free trial signups (5-day trial) from professional services decision-makers in Belgium and the Netherlands, with a secondary objective of converting trial users into paying subscribers at €19+/month.

### Business Context
Professional services firms — architects, consultants, accountants, lawyers, HR advisors — share a universal problem: they spend disproportionate time answering repetitive client questions that do not generate billable income. not-a-bot.ai addresses this directly with a no-hallucination AI agent trained on the firm's own documents. The €19/month price point is below any professional service firm's coffee budget — the barrier is awareness and perceived setup complexity, not price.

### KPI Framework

| Funnel Stage | Primary KPI | Target | Secondary KPI | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Reach (unique users) | 30,000–60,000 | CPM | <€14 |
| Consideration | CTR | >0.6% (LinkedIn), >1.5% (Meta) | Landing page dwell time | >45 sec |
| Conversion | Trials started | 80–120 in 4 weeks | CPL (cost per trial) | €35–55 |
| Quality | Trial-to-MQL conversion | 20–30% | MQL-to-SQL (sales qualified) | 15–25% |
| Revenue | Paid conversions (post-trial) | 15–25 new subscribers | CAC | <€120 |

### Campaign CPL Target Rationale
At €19/month/agent and a conservative 8-month average customer lifetime, LTV ≈ €152. A CPL of €35–55 yields a CAC:LTV ratio of approximately 1:3 — healthy for a SaaS product with near-zero support cost per customer in the first 3 months.

- Checks LinkedIn 3–4x per week, primarily mobile, during commute or lunch
- Uses Google to research specific problems ("how to automate client questions")

**Jobs to Be Done (JTBD):**

1. "AI will give wrong answers" — addressed by no-hallucination guarantee

## 3. Key Messages Per Funnel Stage

### Awareness Stage — "This is your problem, exactly"

The goal at awareness is recognition, not conversion. The prospect must see themselves in the message. Copy should describe the specific pain (repetitive client FAQ, inbox management, intake processes) in concrete terms, not abstract AI benefits.

**Primary message:** Professional firms lose 5–15 hours per month answering questions they've already answered. This is not a people problem. It's a systems problem.

**Supporting messages:**
- "Every hour you spend on email repetition is an hour you're not billing"
- "Your expertise is not in your inbox — but your clients are getting it there for free"
- "AI doesn't have to mean complex. 28 minutes. No developer. €19/month."

**Tone:** Direct, peer-level. Not sales-y. Slightly provocative. Like a colleague who's found a better way.

---

### Consideration Stage — "Here's exactly how it works"

Prospects in consideration have self-identified with the problem. Now they need to understand the mechanism (document training, no hallucinations), the effort (28 minutes, no IT), and the cost (€19/month). Specificity is the key persuasion lever here — vague benefit claims do not move professionals.

**Primary message:** You upload your service brochure, FAQ, and process documents. The agent learns from them. Your clients ask questions. The agent answers — from your documents only.

**Supporting messages:**
- "No hallucinations" as a differentiator from generic AI tools (explicit and repeated)
- Case study metrics: "Studio Merckx reduced intake questions by 60% in month 1"
- "Live in 28 minutes" with a visual walkthrough (how-it-works video or step graphic)
- GDPR compliance and European hosting for risk-averse professional service buyers

**Tone:** Informative, specific, confident. Show the product — but show its outcome first.

---

### Decision Stage — "Try it and see — zero risk"

At decision stage, the primary barrier is inertia and perceived risk, not awareness or understanding. The prospect likely already wants the product. The job of decision-stage copy is to remove the reason not to act right now.

**Primary message:** Free 5-day trial. No credit card. Cancel in one click. You have nothing to lose except the time you spend on repetitive emails.

**Supporting messages:**
- "Your first agent could be live by end of day"
- "If the trial doesn't work, you walk away — no commitment, no invoice"
- Social proof at scale: "500+ businesses started their trial this year"
- Urgency frame (not fake): "High season (for vacation rental) / Q3 intake (for consultants) is approaching. Set it up now, not when you're already busy."

**Tone:** Reassuring, confident, low-pressure. Remove anxiety; don't add it.

4. Trust strip: logos or names of reference customers (Studio Merckx, etc.) + "500+ businesses"

**Required page elements (below fold):**
- How it works: 3-step visual (Upload → Configure → Go live)

- Objection FAQ:
  - "Will it give wrong answers?" — No. It only answers from your documents.
  - "What if my questions are too complex?" — Complex ones forward to your team, with context.
  - "Is my data safe?" — GDPR compliant, European infrastructure.
- Secondary CTA: "Calculate your time savings" (ROI calculator, if available)
- Final CTA: repeated "Start free trial" button

**LP quality requirements:**
- Load time: <2 seconds (LCP)
- Mobile-optimised layout (primary audience is mobile)
- H1 must match the main campaign headline (Quality Score + relevance)
- No navigation menu during trial signup flow (remove distractions on conversion path)
- Form: first name + work email only (minimise friction — no phone number, no company name at signup stage)

## 7. Email Follow-Up Sequence

Reference the existing Lead→MQL nurture flow. For Professional Services trial users, the following sequence applies:

**Trigger:** Trial signup confirmed (CompleteRegistration event)

**Email 1 — Day 0 (immediate, post-signup):**
Subject: "Your not-a-bot.ai agent is ready to train"
Preview: "Here's how to go live in the next 28 minutes."
Content: Setup guide, first document to upload (suggest starting with service FAQ or brochure), video link (2-min walkthrough), primary CTA: "Upload your first document now"
Tone: Warm, practical, energising

**Email 2 — Day 2:**
Subject: "How Studio Merckx saved 3 hours in week 1"
Preview: "One document. One agent. 60% fewer intake emails."
Content: Mini case study. Architect/consultant context. Show before/after metrics. Include specific questions their agent now handles automatically. CTA: "Add a second document to your agent" (progression nudge)
Tone: Peer storytelling, specific and credible

**Email 3 — Day 4 (day before trial expires):**
Subject: "Your trial ends tomorrow. Here's what's happened."
Preview: "X questions answered by your agent this week."
Content: Dynamic if possible — show actual usage stats from their 5-day trial. If usage is low: re-invite to complete setup (include video link again). CTA: "Start your paid plan — €19/month" and secondary: "Need help? Book a 15-minute call."
Tone: Direct, honest, low-pressure. Not fear-based.

**Email 4 — Day 7 (post-trial, for non-converters):**
Subject: "Did something get in the way?"
Preview: "Your trial ended, but your questions didn't."
Content: Short. One question: "What stopped you?" — 3-button survey (Too complex / Too expensive / Not the right time). Based on response, branch to:
- Too complex → Schedule onboarding call
- Too expensive → Nothing (€19/month — hold position; do not discount)
- Not the right time → Re-subscribe at 60 days with re-activation offer
CTA: "Return to not-a-bot.ai — your documents are still saved"

**Email 5 — Day 30 (win-back, for non-converters who selected "not the right time"):**
Subject: "It's been a month. Your repetitive emails haven't stopped."
Preview: "5 minutes to reconsider."
Content: BAB structure — before (still in inbox), after (where they could be), bridge (€19/month, 5-day trial available again). CTA: "Start a new free trial"

## 8. Success Metrics and Optimisation Triggers

### Weekly Review Cadence

**Every Monday:**
- Pull CTR, CPM, CPC, CPL per channel and per variant
- Flag any metric deviating >25% from target (up or down)
- Check conversion tracking integrity (confirm trial starts are recording correctly)

**End of Week 2:**
- Pause any ad variant with CTR below floor threshold (0.35% LinkedIn; 0.8% Meta; 2% Google)
- Identify top performer per channel — begin concentrating budget

**End of Week 3:**
- A/B winner declared per channel (statistical confidence: 80% minimum, 95% preferred with sufficient volume)
- Landing page conversion rate review — if LP CVR <5%, LP quality is the bottleneck (not the ads)
- Email sequence open rate review — if Day 2 open <30%, test alternative subject line

**End of Week 4:**
- Full campaign report (see Section 10 for A/B test plan deliverable)
- Month 2 recommendation: scale (top performers), refresh creative, or reposition

### Optimisation Decision Triggers

| Trigger | Signal | Action |
|---|---|---|
| CTR too low | <0.35% LinkedIn after 3,000 impressions | Swap intro text; test emoji-free vs. stat-led opening |
| CPL too high | >€80 after €200 spend | Narrow audience, review LP conversion rate |
| High CTR, low trial conversion | CTR >1%, trial CVR <3% | LP alignment audit — headline mismatch? |
| Email Day 3 open <25% | Below benchmark | A/B test subject line (add specificity or name) |
| Trial-to-paid <15% | Below target | Product team review — is the trial experience compelling? |
| Google Quality Score <6 | Ad group level | Audit keyword-copy-LP chain; tighten or split ad group |

## 9. Creative Brief — 3 Ad Concepts

### Concept 1: "The 10pm Email"

**Channels:** Meta (video), LinkedIn (static variant)
**Funnel stage:** Awareness — emotional recognition

**Core idea:** A professional service business owner answers the same client email at 10pm on a Tuesday. The moment of recognition: this has happened before. It will happen again. There is a better way.

**Visual direction:**
A single scene. Architect or consultant — 40s, relaxed professional style — at a kitchen table, laptop open. Low light. Wine glass nearby. Phone buzzes with email notification. They open it. We see the screen: "Quick question — what does your onboarding process look like?" They sigh, slightly — not dramatically — and start typing. Below the scene, in clean white text: "You've answered this before."

Cut to: same setting, six months later. Same person. Same laptop. No email buzzing. Phone shows: "3 messages handled by your agent today." They close the laptop. Pour the wine. Live their life.

Tagline: "Stop explaining. Start billing."
CTA: "28 minutes to set up. Try free."

**Execution notes:**
- Shoot in a real home-office or kitchen environment. No studio sets.
- Talent: cast authentically — someone who looks like an architect in their 40s, not a stock-photo professional
- Video: 30–40 seconds. Vertical (9:16) for Reels, also export 1:1 for Feed.
- No voice-over required — on-screen text carries the narrative. Subtle sound design.

---

### Concept 2: "The ROI Conversation"

**Channels:** LinkedIn (static), Google Display (if applicable)
**Funnel stage:** Consideration — rational justification

**Core idea:** A clean, data-driven visual that gives professional service buyers the permission to buy. Not "look how amazing this product is" — but "look at what your time is actually costing you."

**Visual direction:**
A simple infographic layout. Left column: "What your inbox is costing you."
- 40 FAQ emails per month
- 15 minutes to answer each
- = 10 hours per month
- At €100/hour billing rate = €1,000/month in unrecovered time

Right column: "What not-a-bot.ai costs you."
- €19/month
- 28 minutes to set up
- 68% of those questions automated

Bottom row: "You make back month 1 in 20 minutes."

Design: clean white background, brand blue for numbers, thin grid lines. No photography. Data confidence, not lifestyle aspiration.

**Execution notes:**
- This creative is designed for consideration-stage LinkedIn feed (no animation needed)
- Also effective as a Google Display asset for retargeting
- Keep the math simple and visible — no small print
- Include a footnote: "Based on not-a-bot.ai customer data, BE/NL, 2025"

---

### Concept 3: "What Your Agent Handles"

**Channels:** Meta (carousel), LinkedIn (document ad or carousel)
**Funnel stage:** Consideration to Decision — specificity as trust

**Core idea:** Show exactly what kinds of questions a professional services AI agent handles. Not generic AI promises — specific, real-world questions from the target sector.

**Visual direction:**
A carousel. Each card = one real question that the agent answers automatically.

Card 1: "What's your availability for a project starting in September?"
Card 2: "Can you send me a cost estimate for a 200m² renovation?"
Card 3: "Do you work with residential clients or only commercial?"
Card 4: "What does your typical onboarding process look like?"
Card 5: "How long does a full brand strategy project usually take?"

Each card: question text in large font (as if from an email or WhatsApp). Below: "Your agent handles this automatically." Small: "(from your documents, no hallucinations)"

Final card: "How many of these are in your inbox right now? Try free — 28 minutes."

**Execution notes:**
- Cards should look exactly like a real email or chat message — use authentic messaging UI style
- The realism is the persuasion mechanism — the reader recognises their own inbox
- LinkedIn: consider Document Ad format (PDF carousel) for extended dwell time
- Instagram: standard carousel

## 10. A/B Test Plan

### Test Structure — Priority Order

| Test # | Channel | Variable | Control | Challenger | Success Metric | Minimum Sample |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | LinkedIn | Ad copy approach | A1 — Problem-focused | A2 — Social proof | CTR | 3,000 impressions per variant |
| 2 | LinkedIn | Intro text style | Stat-led ("40 times this month") | Question-led ("Still answering the same questions?") | CTR | 3,000 impressions per variant |
| 3 | Meta | Creative format | Static image (TOF3) | Video (TOF1) | CPL (LP visits) | 500 LP visits per variant |
| 4 | Google | RSA headline approach | Problem-centric (Variant 1) | Outcome-first (Variant 3) | CTR + trial CVR | 100 clicks per variant |
| 5 | All channels | Landing page headline | "Stop explaining. Start billing." | "Your AI agent handles client FAQ in 28 minutes." | Landing page CVR | 200 LP visits per variant |
| 6 | Email (Day 3) | Subject line | "Your trial ends tomorrow" | "X questions answered by your agent — here's a recap" | Open rate | 200 sends per variant |

### Test Governance Rules

1. Run only one test per channel at a time (do not A/B test creative AND audience simultaneously — results become unreadable)
2. Declare winner at 80% statistical confidence minimum; wait for 95% before scaling budget
3. Document every test: hypothesis, result, date, winning variant, reason for win (hypothesis)
4. Do not pause a test after 24 hours — Meta and LinkedIn algorithms need 3–5 days to distribute fairly before drawing conclusions
5. Keep a test log in the campaign folder — include both winners and losers (losing data is valuable)

### End-of-Campaign Test Report Deliverables
- Winning creative per channel with metrics
- Losing creative with reason for loss (copy, audience, timing, or format)
- Recommended month 2 creative based on learnings
- Recommendation on budget reallocation between channels based on CPL comparison
